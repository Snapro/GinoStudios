What Zambian Brands Get Wrong About Visual Identity — And How to Fix It

A brand identity is not a logo. It is a system. Here is what the strongest brand identities in Zambia's market have in common — and what the weakest ones are missing.

The Logo Misconception

The most pervasive misunderstanding about brand identity in Zambia — and in most markets — is the belief that a logo equals a brand identity. It does not. A logo is a mark. A brand identity is a complete visual system: the logo, certainly, but also a colour palette with defined values for both print and screen, a typography hierarchy with specific typefaces and weight assignments, a photography style that defines how the brand appears in imagery, an iconography style, and a set of spatial and layout principles that determine how all of these elements are assembled in practice.

Why Systems Matter

Without a complete system, every piece of communication a brand produces will look slightly different from the last. Different shades of the brand colour, different fonts used interchangeably, different photography styles across collateral — each inconsistency is a small erosion of trust. Audiences recognise consistency as a signal of care and professionalism. Inconsistency communicates carelessness, even when the underlying product or service is excellent.

The Common Gaps

In our work across Zambia's business landscape, the most common gaps we see in brand identities are: unclear colour system (no defined CMYK and RGB values, leading to inconsistency between print and digital), no defined typography hierarchy (leading to random font use across materials), photography style that does not match the brand's positioning (stock images that feel generic or inconsistent with the brand's market position), and no spatial system (no guidance on margins, spacing, or layout principles).

Where to Start

A brand audit is the most useful first step for any business that suspects its visual identity is holding it back. This means gathering every piece of communication the business has produced — business cards, social media posts, proposals, signage — and looking at them together. Do they look like they come from the same brand? If not, where are the inconsistencies, and what do they communicate about how the business perceives itself? This honest assessment is the foundation of a meaningful brand identity project. If you would like a fresh perspective on your brand's visual system, we would be glad to help.
